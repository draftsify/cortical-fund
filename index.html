          What is Cortical Fund?
          
Cortical Fund is a token. Trading it generates pool fees, and the intent of this project is to periodically claim those fees and contribute them to Cortical Labs, the company building CL1 — a computer that runs on living human neurons cultured on a silicon chip.

        

        

          How do trading fees turn into contributions?
          
Every trade carries a 1% pool fee, which the launchpad splits between the token's creator and the platform. The fund receives the creator's 70% share. That share is claimed weekly, converted to stablecoins, and wired out. The claim and the transfer are both published in the ledger.

        

        

          Why Cortical Labs?
          
Because synthetic biological intelligence is one of the few compute frontiers that isn't already flooded with capital. Neurons learn from vastly less data and run on a fraction of the energy of silicon. Cortical Labs put the first commercial biological computer in the world on the market — that work deserves a funding stream that doesn't depend on a quarterly cycle.

        

        

          What are the risks of buying the token?
          
The token can lose value, including all of its value. Buying it is not a donation, it is not tax-deductible, and it gives you no claim on the fund, on Cortical Labs, or on anything else. If fees stop, contributions stop. Assume you may lose everything you put in.

        

        

          Do holders receive anything in return?
          
No. Holding $CTX does not entitle you to dividends, revenue share, redemption rights, equity, hardware, or any say in how the project is run. The only thing it produces is fees, and the fees are not yours.

        

        

          What is the contract address?
